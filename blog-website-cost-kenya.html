How much does a business website cost in Kenya in 2027?

November 16, 2026 · 7 min read

Ask five web designers in Nairobi what a website costs and you will get five very different answers, from KES 15,000 to well over KES 300,000. Both can be fair prices, because they are not the same product. This guide explains what drives the price, what you should expect to pay in Kenya going into 2027, and what to check before you hand over a deposit.

The short answer

Looking at prices Kenyan web designers and agencies publish, here is roughly what businesses pay to have a website designed and built:

  • Simple business website (1 to 10 pages): about KES 20,000 to KES 120,000
  • Site with bookings, enquiry automation, or M-Pesa payments: about KES 60,000 to KES 250,000
  • Online shop (e-commerce): about KES 50,000 to KES 350,000, depending on the number of products and payment options
  • Custom web application or portal: usually from KES 100,000 upwards

On top of the build, you pay every year for a domain name and hosting, and usually something for upkeep. Those are covered below.

For comparison, our own websites start from KES 50,000, including VAT, for a custom-designed site of up to 5 pages, mobile responsive and SEO optimised, with enquiry forms and calendar booking built in. Why prices vary so much

A KES 15,000 website and a KES 150,000 website are usually different in five ways:

  • Template or custom design. A ready-made theme with your logo dropped in is quick and cheap. A design built around your business, your customers, and what you want them to do costs more, and usually converts better.
  • Who writes the content. Many low quotes assume you supply all the text and photos. Writing, editing, and organising content is real work and is often where projects stall.
  • Speed and SEO. A site that loads fast on a phone using mobile data, has proper page titles, and is set up for Google takes more care than one that only looks good on the designer's laptop.
  • What it connects to. Booking calendars, M-Pesa, WhatsApp, a CRM, or automatic replies to enquiries all add work, but they are also what turn a website from a brochure into something that brings in business.
  • Who you are paying. A freelancer, a small studio, and a large agency all price differently. None is automatically better; what matters is whether they have built sites like yours before.

The yearly costs people forget

The build is a one-time cost, but every website also has running costs:

  • Domain name: a .co.ke or .com usually costs around KES 1,000 to KES 2,000 a year.
  • Hosting: shared hosting for a small business site is often KES 1,500 to KES 10,000 a year. Busy sites, online shops, and web apps need more.
  • Business email: addresses like you@yourbusiness.co.ke are sometimes included with hosting, or charged per person each month through Google Workspace or Microsoft 365.
  • Upkeep: updates, security fixes, backups, and small changes. Some designers charge per change; others offer a monthly plan.

Always check the renewal price of the domain and hosting, not just the first year. Promotional prices often double when they renew.

Make sure you own your website

This is the most common problem we see in Kenya. A business pays for a website, but the domain is registered in the designer's name, the hosting is on the designer's account, and when the relationship ends the business loses its website, its email, or both. Before you pay, agree in writing that:

  • The domain is registered in your business's name, and you have the login
  • You have access to the hosting account, or the designer gives it to you at handover
  • You receive the website's files and any design files when the project is paid for

Six questions to ask before you pay a web designer

  1. Can I see sites you have built, and visit them live? Screenshots are not enough. Open them on your phone.
  2. Exactly what is included in the price? Number of pages, who writes the content, revisions, and whether domain and hosting are included.
  3. How fast will it load on a phone? Most of your visitors in Kenya will be on mobile data.
  4. Will it be set up for Google? Page titles, descriptions, Google Search Console, and a Google Business Profile link.
  5. Who owns the domain, hosting, and files? See the section above.
  6. What happens after launch? What changes cost, how quickly they are made, and who to call when something breaks.

A website should bring in business, not just look good

Most business websites in Kenya are digital brochures. Someone finds you, reads a page, and then has to call, send a WhatsApp, or fill in a form and wait. Many never do. The websites that pay for themselves make the next step easy: an enquiry gets an instant reply, the visitor can book a meeting or a visit straight into your calendar, and they can pay by M-Pesa or card without leaving the page. If you are reaching prospects by cold calls, email, or ads, that is the difference between a site that supports your sales and one that quietly loses them.

Common questions

How much does a business website cost in Kenya?

Going by prices Kenyan web designers publish, a simple business website of up to about 10 pages usually costs between KES 20,000 and KES 120,000 to design and build. Online shops and sites with booking or M-Pesa payments cost more, often KES 60,000 to KES 300,000 or above. Clavyra websites start from KES 50,000, including VAT.

How much are a domain and hosting in Kenya?

A .co.ke or .com domain usually costs around KES 1,000 to 2,000 a year, and shared hosting for a small business site is often KES 1,500 to 10,000 a year. Check the renewal price, because it is often higher than the first-year price.

Can my website take M-Pesa payments?

Yes. The simplest option is showing your Paybill or Till number. An automatic M-Pesa prompt, where the customer gets a PIN request on their phone, needs an integration with Safaricom and adds to the cost.

How long does it take to build a website in Kenya?

A simple business website usually takes two to four weeks once your content is ready. Sites with online payments, bookings, or many pages take longer. Waiting for text and photos is the most common cause of delays.
